Learn Personal Branding with Phoebe · Session 6 of 6

A system you keep

Most personal-branding efforts end the same way: a burst of posts in a motivated month, then silence, then a profile that is older than the person's current job. The fix is not more motivation. It is a small system that runs on the hours you actually have: a cadence sized to your week rather than to someone's advice, an idea bank that refills from work you are already doing, and a review every quarter that re-runs the search audit from session 1 so you can see, in two counts, whether anything changed. Tonight you build that system on one page and put twelve weeks of it in a calendar.

Part 1 · covers cadence you can sustain

Size the cadence to the hours, not the advice

5 min live

Session 4 measured what posting more buys on real rows: in a creator's busier blocks they earn more total score that block (r 0.660), but not more per answer (r -0.005), and a busy block does not lift the next one (r 0.004). Cadence is a volume dial. That makes the question practical rather than heroic: how many pieces of the depth that earns a result can you make in the hours you really have, every week, for twelve weeks?

LivePick the cadence you can keep for twelve weeks3 min▶

Start from the hours, not from a number of posts. Write down the hours you can protect in an ordinary week, not a good one. Estimate how long one piece at the depth that earns a result takes you: a teardown, a worked example, a considered answer. Divide. The result is your cadence, and it is usually smaller than the advice.

Then cut it by a third for the weeks that go wrong. A cadence you keep through a bad month beats a better one you abandon, because session 4 found no sign that a burst grows the next block, and the per-piece value of what you publish does not rise with the count.

Self-study"Stick around"2 min▶

The last chapter title of Austin Kleon's Show Your Work! is "Stick around". It is the honest summary of what the bench can and cannot say. The bench cannot show that any cadence grows an audience; it can show that the creators who were still answering at the end are the ones every success chart is drawn from (51 of 302). Staying is the precondition for being in that set at all. A small cadence is how people stay.

The disagreement from session 4 still stands: Buffer's LinkedIn analysis found more posts went with more reach per post on a feed platform. If your outpost is a feed, test it on your own numbers for a quarter rather than adopting either finding.

Real world

Ines (constructed) planned five a week in session 3. Her protected time is four hours a week and a teardown takes her about two (both constructed). Five a week would leave 48 minutes a piece, not enough for the work that makes her line true. She sets two: one teardown, one short share of something she learned, every week, with a skip allowed once a month.

Part 2 · covers the idea bank

An idea bank that refills from your own work

5 min live

The week a cadence breaks is the week there is nothing to say. The fix is a running list that fills itself from things that happen anyway, and a short triage that turns an entry into the next piece. If the bank is ever empty, the inflow is wrong, not you.

LiveFour inflows that never run dry3 min▶

Questions you get asked. If a colleague asked it, someone in your audience is wondering it. Work you finished. Every finished piece is a teardown candidate once anything confidential is removed (session 3). Mistakes you fixed. The most useful public writing in most technical fields is a mistake explained by the person who made it. Reading you disagreed with. A disagreement with a source, stated fairly, is a point of view, which is what a positioning line needs behind it.

Capture is one line, at the moment, in one place. A bank spread across four apps is four banks, and the empty week is the week you cannot find any of them.

Self-studyWhen to say no to a good idea2 min▶

The triage exists to protect the line from session 2. An idea that is interesting but does not serve the audience in your line is a good idea for somebody else. An idea you cannot back with a proof is a claim, which session 3 moved you away from. Neither is deleted; both go back with a note. Some of them become right when your work catches up with them.

Turning one idea into several formats is a production skill, taught in AI Content ↗. Here the job is only choosing which idea is next.

Part 3 · covers the review

The quarterly re-audit

4 min live

A system needs a way to tell whether it is working that does not depend on how you feel about it. You already have one: the dated search audit from session 1. Re-run it word for word every twelve weeks, take the same two counts, and the before and after are evidence, because both carry the date they were taken.

LiveRe-run session 1 word for word2 min▶

Same two searches, same private signed-out window, same ten slots, same four letters, same two counts. Change nothing about the method, or the comparison measures your method instead of your reputation. Then answer three questions in writing: which new result arrived and from which piece, which old result is still wrong, and what the next quarter's one added result will be.

Search results move slowly and for reasons you do not control. A quarter with no change is a data point, not a verdict; two quarters with no change on the second count is a sign that the work is not being published where a search can see it, which sends you back to the platform map from session 5.

Self-studyWhat to stop2 min▶

Every review should remove something. An outpost nobody in your audience reads, a format that takes twice the hours of the others, a recurring piece that never produced a reply. Stopping is how a small system stays small enough to keep. Write the stopped item in the review with the reason, so a later quarter does not restart it out of guilt.

For managers coaching a team: run the review together, once a quarter, as a short working session. Each person brings their two counts and one result they are proud of. Nobody is compared with anybody else; session 4 showed what cross-person comparisons do to a reading of cadence.

Part 4 · the final scorecard

Eight checks before you call it a system

4 min live

A plan describes what you intend. A system is what still happens in week nine when the week goes wrong. Score the one you have tonight, not the one you mean to build.

I have a dated search audit with two counts, and the re-audit date is in my calendar twelve weeks from tonight. Sessions 1 and 6. Without the date, the before and after are not evidence.
My positioning line passes the competitor swap, and I have read its four counts in the checker without trusting the percentage. Session 2. A line another hundred people could sign is a category, not a niche.
At least one piece of my real work is public, on a page with my name in the title, with nothing confidential in it. Session 3. Rung 3 of the proof ladder: something a stranger can open and judge.
My cadence comes from my protected hours divided by the time one piece takes me, cut by a third for bad weeks. Sessions 4 and 6. On the bench, a busy block bought volume (r 0.660) and nothing per answer (r -0.005).
I have one owned home and no more than two rented outposts, and every outpost post points home. Session 5. Rented ground can shrink under everyone; the bench's whole site fell from 7.36 to 0.82 score per answer.
My idea bank has at least ten one-line entries, in one place, from at least three of the four inflows. Session 6. The empty week is the week a cadence breaks.
I know which claim I will never make in public, and every number I quote has a source I could produce. Sessions 1 and 3. The unsourced quote and the unsourced statistic are the same mistake.
I have written down one thing I will stop doing, with the reason. Session 6. A system stays keepable by getting smaller at every review.

Homework

The next twelve weeks

ongoing
  1. Publish week 1's teardown on the page with your name in its title and link it from the profile you keep.
  2. Add to the idea bank the moment something happens, not at the end of the week.
  3. At week 6, cut a slot if you are behind. A smaller cadence kept is the system working.
  4. At week 12, re-run the audit word for word and fill the review form. Then start the next twelve weeks with one thing stopped.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Ines (constructed) has four protected hours a week and a teardown takes her two. What cadence does the system give her, and why not five?

A reads the cross-creator chart, which compares different people. The cadence comes from the hours divided by the depth, and the bench says the extra volume would buy nothing that compounds. C and D are slogans without a sum behind them.

2 · Why must the quarterly re-audit repeat session 1 word for word?

A dated measurement repeated the same way is the only thing that turns "I think it is working" into a number you can check. It is the same reason the bench holds the rows fixed and changes only the view.

3 · Your idea bank is empty in week 5. What does the system say is wrong?

The bank is fed by events that already happen. An empty bank is a capture problem, fixed by one place and one line at the moment, not a talent problem.
