# Official course map - learn-personal-branding-with-phoebe

Built 2026-10-05. Bucket biz, difficulty 2, audience both (people building a professional
reputation, and the managers and mentors who coach them). Single track, six 45-minute sessions.
Session 4 is the computed bench. Every number on every page must come from this file; anything
missing is taught as uncertain, never invented.

## Hard rule: the running case is FICTIONAL

**Ines Calloway (constructed).** A mid-career data analyst, eight years in, currently doing demand
forecasting at a regional grocery chain (constructed). She wants a public reputation that makes the
right people find her. Every page labels her "constructed" at least once. She is not Phoebe, not
modelled on Phoebe, and no page uses Phoebe's own journey, profiles, courses, gallery, employers or
clients as an example. Her search audit, drafts, ideas and calendar are all constructed and pages
say "your results will differ".

Constructed artefacts reused across pages (keep identical everywhere):
- Search audit of her name, first page, 10 results (constructed): 3 are a namesake (a ceramicist in
  another country), 2 are her own (an old conference speaker page, a dormant LinkedIn profile),
  1 is a company staff page that still lists her old job title, 4 are unrelated. So 2 of 10 are
  hers and current-ish; 0 show any work.
- Draft positioning lines (exact text, scored by the session 2 widget):
  - Draft 1: "Passionate, results-driven data enthusiast helping businesses unlock insights and drive growth." -> 11 words, audience 0, outcome 0, proof 0, vague 7, specificity 0%.
  - Draft 2: "Data analyst who builds forecasting dashboards for retail teams." -> 9 words, audience 1, outcome 0, proof 0, vague 0, specificity 100% (the heuristic counted "analyst", which names HER, not her audience: the counts matter more than the percentage).
  - Draft 3: "I help grocery planners cut stockouts with forecasts they can check, one public teardown at a time." -> 17 words, audience 2, outcome 4, proof 1, vague 0, specificity 100%. (Revised 2026-10-05: an earlier draft claimed "14 public teardowns so far", which contradicted session 1's 0-of-10 audit and session 3's first teardown.)
  - Test line: "Seasoned, strategic thought leader and lifelong learner." -> 7 words, vague 4.
  - Test line: "Helping 3 warehouse teams find late orders before 9am." -> 9 words, audience 1, outcome 2, proof 2.

## Per-session coverage

1. **A reputation is a search result** (`01-a-reputation-is-a-search-result.html`, difficulty
   green). What a stranger finds when they look you up; the first page as the real CV; four kinds
   of result (yours and owned, yours on rented ground, about you, not you); namesakes; Google's
   "Results about you" tool and what it can and cannot do; Tom Peters 1997 (secondary); the
   "what people say when you're not in the room" line and why its Bezos attribution is unverified.
   Build-along: the ten-result search audit on your own name.
2. **Your niche: the onliness test** (`02-the-onliness-test.html`, yellow). Positioning for one
   person; Neumeier's onliness statement (concept, wording reported); the competitor-swap test;
   audience, outcome, proof; Kelly's 1,000 True Fans as the arithmetic of a small niche (read at
   source, both framings, and the Lanier challenge). Second widget: the positioning-line scorer
   (heuristic). Build-along: three drafts to one line.
3. **Voice and proof: show the work** (`03-show-the-work.html`, yellow). Kleon's ten chapter
   titles (read at source on his site); proof over claims; the proof ladder (claim < description <
   artefact you can open < artefact someone else used); voice as consistent choices, not
   adjectives. Build-along: turn one piece of real work into a public artefact (a teardown).
4. **The consistency bench** (`04-the-consistency-bench.html`, red, bench night). The computed bench on
   real rows; "post more" tested; survivor trap; within vs across; break button; Buffer's
   contrary within-account finding taught as the disagreement.
5. **Platforms and formats: owned and rented ground** (`05-owned-and-rented-ground.html`,
   orange). Where your audience reads; owned vs rented audience; Carr's digital sharecropping
   (2006, read in the 2011 rerelease); Copyblogger 2015; format follows the reader; the platform
   decline the bench measured (site score per answer 7.36 in 2014 to 0.82 in 2023) as the rented
   ground shifting under you. Build-along: a one-page platform map.
6. **A system you keep** (`06-a-system-you-keep.html`, orange). Cadence you can sustain (the bench
   says volume, not growth), the idea bank, the quarterly review against the search audit,
   Kleon's "Stick around". Build-along: the one-page operating system and a 12-week calendar.

## Verified facts and evidence tiers

Tiers: **A** read at source (primary page fetched and read). **B** read at source but the source
is company data or self-reported, so the claim is the company's. **R** reported only (secondary
summaries; never quoted as verbatim). **U** unverified / no primary found (teach as contested).

### Session 1
- Google Search Help, "Results about you" (support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076). **A.**
  Lets you find whether personal contact info (home address, phone number, email) shows up in
  results; Google "may approve requests to remove results"; removal "doesn't mean that the info is
  gone from the internet"; notifications when new results match; no remove option for pages Google
  deems valuable to the public. It is a privacy tool, not a reputation editor.
- Tom Peters, "The Brand Called You", Fast Company, August 1997 issue. **R** (Fast Company
  returned 403; Peters' own site, tompeters.com/brand-1997-remembered/, says the piece was edited
  from a phone interview by Alan Webber). No verbatim quote from the article on any page.
- "Your brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room", attributed to Jeff
  Bezos. **U.** No primary source located; Quote Investigator has no entry; wording varies.
  Teach as "widely attributed, never sourced". The idea also echoes Neumeier's line that a brand is
  what they say it is (R, wording not verified, do not quote).

### Session 2
- Marty Neumeier, "Zag" (2006), onliness statement. **R.** Concept: a brand should be able to say
  it is the only one that does a thing; the reported minimal template is "Our [offering] is the
  only [category] that [benefit]". Exact Neumeier wording not verified: pages present the template
  as "reported" and never in quotation marks as his words. The competitor-swap test (swap in a
  rival's name; if the line still works, it is not yours) is taught as a practitioner test, not
  attributed to Neumeier.
- Kevin Kelly, "1,000 True Fans", kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/, dated March 4, 2008. **A.**
  The page carries a shorter rewrite on top (appeared in Tim Ferriss' "Tools of Titans"; year not
  on the page) and the 2008 original below. Rewrite: a true fan "will buy anything you produce";
  $100 PROFIT per fan per year, 1,000 fans = $100,000 a year. Original 2008: $100 SPENT per fan per
  year (one day's wages). Do not merge the two framings. "The number 1,000 is not absolute" ($50 a
  fan needs 2,000, $200 needs 500). Requires a direct relationship ("they must pay you directly").
  Kelly himself: "My formula may be off by an order of magnitude". Follow-up "The Case Against
  1000 True Fans" (kk.org, April 27, 2008, **A**): Jaron Lanier says he has not found a single
  musician meeting the definition. Teach: the arithmetic is a model of a small direct niche, its
  author flagged its uncertainty, and the challenge is on his own site.
- Positioning-line scorer: heuristic counts above (python and node agree on all six test lines).

### Session 3
- Austin Kleon, "Show Your Work!" (Workman, 2014), chapter titles from austinkleon.com/show-your-work/.
  **A** (relayed by fetch; re-grep before quoting a sentence). The ten: You don't have to be a
  genius · Think process, not product · Share something small every day · Open up your cabinet of
  curiosities · Tell good stories · Teach what you know · Don't turn into human spam · Learn to take
  a punch · Sell out · Stick around. Chapter titles may be listed; no prose from the book quoted.
- The proof ladder and voice-as-choices are the course's own frameworks, taught as such.

### Session 4 (bench canon, computed; python and node agree on every value to 6 decimals)
Data: Data Science Stack Exchange public data dump, April 2024 (archive.org/details/stackexchange,
file datascience.stackexchange.com.7z, last-modified 2024-04-07). Licence CC BY-SA (2.5 / 3.0 /
4.0 by post date; attribution: indicate Stack Exchange as the source). Derived counts shared under
CC BY-SA 4.0; no text, names or ids shipped. 302 accounts with 20+ answers, 22,657 answers, first
2014-05, last 2024-03-31, cut into 28-day blocks from 2014-05-12 (129 blocks, 0 to 128).
Engagement = answer score (upvotes minus downvotes) at dump time.

| View | n | Pearson r | Spearman | Other measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Across creators, score per week | 302 | 0.584 | 0.802 | busiest third 1.68 answers/wk earn 3.13 score/wk; quietest third 0.15/wk earn 0.34/wk |
| Across creators, score per answer | 302 | -0.070 | -0.180 | per answer: busiest third 1.85, quietest third 2.24 |
| Survivors only (answered in final 7 blocks) | 51 | 0.876 | 0.870 | survivors' r for per answer -0.127; survivors average 1.32 per answer vs 2.12 for all 302; mean start year 2019.4 vs 2017.8 |
| Same creator, total score per block | 12,203 creator-blocks, 302 creators | 0.660 | 0.785 | mechanical: more answers, more votes |
| Same creator, score per answer | 4,498 creator-blocks, 301 creators | -0.005 | 0.104 | sign depends on the measure; neither is a usable effect |
| Same creator, busy block -> next block per answer | 2,660 pairs, 279 creators | 0.004 | 0.054 | no growth signal |

Break button (shuffle cadence within creator, mulberry32 seeds): default seed 1 gives
within_total -0.010, within_per 0.001, within_growth 0.018. Over seeds 1, 4, 7, 10, 13 the
shuffled within_total r ranges -0.027 to 0.025 (the noise floor).

Site-wide mean score per answer by year (all accounts): 2014 7.36 · 2015 3.86 · 2016 3.62 ·
2017 3.46 · 2018 2.53 · 2019 1.73 · 2020 1.36 · 2021 0.99 · 2022 0.99 · 2023 0.82 · 2024 (Q1) 1.05.
Answers per year peaked at 7,779 in 2019.

Honest limits: Stack Exchange is search-driven Q&A with no follower graph, so "audience growth"
here means score per answer, not followers. Feed-algorithm platforms may behave differently.
Score accumulates over time and site-wide voting fell sharply, so cross-creator comparisons mix
start year with behaviour (the survivor row shows it). 20+ answers is a selection rule.

The disagreement to teach: **Buffer**, "How often should you post on LinkedIn"
(buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/, Tamilore Oladipo, data analysis by Julian
Winternheimer, dated 28 Aug 2025 per fetch, page since re-titled 2026). **B.** 2+ million posts
from 94,000+ accounts; compares each account to its own baseline; reports that moving from 1 to
2-5 posts a week adds impressions per post. Company data on its own users, observational, quality
not isolated. On a feed platform with Buffer's method, more posts went with more per-post reach; on
Stack Exchange per answer, within creator, they did not. Both can be true; neither settles the
other. Buffer's "5x more engagement" claim (social-media-frequency-guide) has no method on the
page: **U**, do not quote. HubSpot "13,500 organisations" figures: **U** (only in secondary
snippets). "Post daily to grow": **U**, no primary source located. LinkedIn's own frequency
guidance: **U**.

### Session 5
- Nicholas Carr, digital sharecropping. Original "Sharecropping the Long Tail", roughtype.com,
  19 Dec 2006; read in the 2011 rerelease (roughtype.com/?p=1524). **A** (via fetch). His point:
  platforms give away the tools and keep ownership of what users make. Carr does NOT say "owned vs
  rented audience"; that is a later marketing gloss.
- Sonia Simone, "Digital Sharecropping", Copyblogger, 28 July 2015. **A.** Building on social
  platforms is building "on someone else's land"; credits the term to Carr.
- Kelly's direct-relationship condition (session 2) is the economic version of the same point.
- Bench: site-wide score per answer 7.36 (2014) to 0.82 (2023): the platform's attention moved,
  not the creators. Rented ground can shrink under you.
- Platform-specific posting-time or algorithm numbers: none verified; pages give none.

### Session 6
- Kleon "Stick around" (A, title). The bench: same-creator busy blocks earn more total score that
  block (r 0.660) but not more per answer (r -0.005) and not more next block (r 0.004): cadence is
  a volume dial, so pick the cadence you can keep, not the one that promises growth.
- Idea bank, review cadence, the 12-week calendar: course frameworks, constructed examples.
